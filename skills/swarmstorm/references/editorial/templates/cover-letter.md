# Editorial template: cover letter

The body of a cover letter for one job posting, written from the candidate's evidence, its
wording drawn from their earlier letters, elected on the parts that make the impression, read by
the hiring manager it is for. Seven seats.

## When

A candidate applies to a posting and hands over: the posting; their evidence (a structured
profile of their works, roles and facts with their constraints, and the CV made for this
application); their earlier letters for roles of the same kind; and, after a first run, their
feedback. The dossier is one per application, kept across its runs.

## The strategy

| field | value | why |
|---|---|---|
| protocol | `editorial`, template `cover-letter` | |
| questions | the body of a cover letter to <company> for <role>, in the posting's language | |
| evidence | `given`: the profile, the CV, the posting, the precedents; `web` for the judge's research only | facts come from the profile and the CV, never from the precedents |
| election | slots `hook`, `core` (the paragraph turning to the posting's core theme), `close`; source `precedents`, each candidate a passage adapted from an earlier letter, its facts from the profile and the CV | the candidate's earlier wordings are ones they already approved; the evidence keeps the facts true |
| altitude | strategic | the hiring manager is owed what, why it matters and what it changed, never the parts |
| iterations | 2 | |
| rounds | 1 | |
| budget | 30 minutes | |
| criteria | the reader shortlists; every claim held by the evidence; the candidate's preferences kept; sounds written for this company | |

## The documents

The dossier is one per application. It adds, inside **Inputs**:

| input | what it is | binding |
|---|---|---|
| the profile | the candidate's works, roles and facts, with their constraints | every fact and constraint binds |
| the CV for this application | a source of proofs | the letter says what the CV leaves out, never restates it |
| the posting | the reader's own document | the reader's own words |
| the precedents | the candidate's earlier letters, closest first | wording and framing only |

and inside **Seats**, the seven seats below.

## The seats

| seat | function | persona and role prompt | effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| judge | judge | the company's recruiter judging the posting: each requirement split into its parts, have · partly · gap, the profile entries that back it, judged from the record alone, never crediting what it does not say; what the posting is really about; the strongest met requirements; the misreadings and their framing | low |
| coach | strategist | the career coach, on the candidate's side, owning the brief: who reads first and how long, the one thing they keep (the candidate's own sentence from the interview binds), the motivation and its weight, tone and form within the candidate's preferences, each work by name (expand · condense · one line · out) with its angle, what is left to the CV and the job interview, each figure with its source field | medium |
| writer | writer | writes the letter as the hiring manager for this role would write it for the candidate they want to hire; every claim from the profile, the CV or the interview | medium |
| recruiter | auditor | the senior recruiter auditing the letter line by line, on the candidate's side: per round one table over every sentence (lacks scope · outcome · verb · aim, priority 1–3, proposal, the fact that backs it), then what only the whole shows; a proposal never outruns its fact; selling and natural language are priority 1 | medium |
| checker | checker | every claim against the profile (its `constraints` included), the CV and the interview's confirmed facts; a claim a constraint forbids is blocking | low |
| screener | reader | the company's hiring manager for this team, tired, having read hundreds of applications this month, giving a letter under a minute; knows only the posting and the letter; verdict scale `shortlist: yes / no / borderline` | low |
| style | style | the posting's voice, tone and energy (its register, warmth, its own vocabulary, the values it names), within the candidate's preferences | low |

**Order of authority**: truth (checker), then the candidate's preferences and feedback, then the
style keeper, then a seat's taste.

## The interview

Before the first run, into the dossier, one question at a time:

1. **The judge's table first**: the posting against the profile, each gap asked as one open
   question about the work done, never offering options; a fact confirmed is written into the
   profile, within its existing fields, dated, on the candidate's yes.
2. **The motivation**: why this company, now; when the candidate cannot phrase it, two or three
   readings drawn from their answer and the record, to choose from.
3. **The one thing the reader keeps**, in the candidate's own sentence.
4. **The preferences, each shown on one sentence written two ways**: voice (first person or
   not), register and warmth, the nod to the company's culture, level of detail, the path's
   starting point, the close. Proposed, confirmed, never assumed:
   - open on the strongest claim, the company's moment in a clause;
   - one closing sentence of value for the company, ending on a strength, no invitation;
   - a personal project without users told by its technical work, not as a product;
   - one warm nod, no pun.
5. **The precedents**: the candidate's earlier letters for roles of the same kind, the closest
   first, at most ten.
6. **The company research**, into the dossier's given research: the company's moment and
   culture, each fact with its source and date.

## The output

The posting's language, the body only, one page, four paragraphs, 250–280 words. The
letter's frame (salutation, closing formula, attachments) is the candidate's, outside the run.

| slot | must do | limit | elected |
|---|---|---|---|
| hook | the strongest claim, the company's moment in a clause, the one warm nod | 2 sentences, ~50 words | yes |
| path | where the candidate's path starts, the works that prove it, the principle they show | ~95 words | no |
| core | the posting's core theme: the candidate's work on it, its traction | ~65 words | yes |
| support and close | the work that supports the core, by its engineering; one sentence of value for the company, ending on a strength | ~65 words, the close ≤ 25 | close |

## What the orchestrator watches

- **A work introduced twice**: each work has one place, the most it does there.
- **Jargon the first reader cannot place**, and dates already said once.
- **A vague hinge** (« le principe est le même »), a colon formula, a process narrated where a
  proof or a concept is owed.
- **A limit read as content**: « still in development », a gap answered, an apology.
- **Accepted wordings** in the dossier's feedback: never struck when used.
- **No example letter written for this posting is an input**: the seats write from the profile,
  the CV and the precedents only.
- **The final text is locked** before it is shown or delivered: the letter rendered is that
  text, verbatim.
